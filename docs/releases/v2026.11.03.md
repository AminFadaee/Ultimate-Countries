## New

- **River maps in Ultimate Iran Geography and Ultimate Netherlands Geography.** The 18 river cards now use the same river maps as Ultimate Places: where the river begins and ends, which way it flows and what lies on the way. Each map is framed on the river itself, at least 40 km across, so short rivers such as the Amstel fill the card, and the deck's cities within 10 km of the river are named. The river's name appears only on the answer side.
- **More source and mouth pictures.** A canal gets a channel with a lock gate (the Aarkanaal for the Amstel), a village part of a town counts as a village (Darfeld for the Vechte), and a river that ends in a desert gets sand dunes (the Hari in the Karakum).
- **Every river map is checked as it is drawn.** The river must fill enough of the card, the source and mouth must touch the line, flow arrows must be well spaced, and no name may cover another, the river or the edge. A map that fails gets the plain map instead.

## Changed

- More rivers show their source. The Brahmaputra starts at the Chemayungdung glacier and flows into the Ganges, the Amur starts where the Shilka and Argun meet, the Orinoco in the Parima Mountains, the Congo at Boyoma Falls, and the Atrek in the Hezar Masjed Mountains (from Persian Wikipedia).
- Sea names at a river's mouth stay inside the card at a readable size (Kara Sea, Caspian Sea, Yenisei Gulf).
- A source range much larger than its river, such as the Zagros, is drawn as a cluster of peaks where the river starts, so the river fills the map.
- The Dez and the Lauwers leave the country decks, because too little of either river can be drawn.
- The River Congo is now called the Congo River, following English Wikipedia.
- The English Channel and the Gulf of Aden show their area. The Atacama Desert's area is now left empty, because it no longer matches its description.
- Ultimate Countries is the same as the previous release.
